
ICICI Bank Limited
Converged Risk Assessment Framework


	•	Overview

Converged Risk Assessment framework outlines the methodology for the assessment of Compliance and Financial Reporting Risk impacting the functioning of the Bank covering the products and processes at the level of Business and/or Operations units and Support functions of the Bank. 
It involves identification of applicable compliance requirements and associated risks, materiality assessment for significant accounts across products and processes, inherent and residual risk assessment and scoring. It further involves use of a converged risk assessment and scoring methodology by obtaining inputs on set parameters from various stakeholders to assess the inherent risk for the assessment unit. The methodology for risk assessment will be followed by the Bank. 
	•	Objectives of the framework

The key objectives of the Converged Risk Assessment framework are as:
	•	Converged Risk Assessment covering Financial Reporting Risk (FRR) and Compliance Risk 
	•	Lay down base procedure for maintenance of a unified repository of risks and controls
	•	Standardise risk assessment procedures and parameters across Compliance and Financial Reporting risk
	•	Facilitate proactive identification and management of regulatory focus areas
	•	Develop comprehensive testing procedures supported through use of analytics
	•	Facilitate ongoing monitoring mechanism along with clearly defined roles and responsibilities across the three lines of defence 
	•	Define procedures for control rationalization thereby reducing the cost of compliance
	•	Enable single view of control environment across the Bank
	•	Lay out the architecture for technology enabled convergent risk assessment

	•	Governance Structure 

The Group Chief Compliance Officer (GCCO) will be the owner of the framework and will be primarily responsible for approval and implementation of the framework. Following are the details of key roles and responsibilities with respect to the framework:
	•	Group Compliance Officer (GCO)
	•	Set ‘tone at the top’ on the need and importance of managing compliance and financial reporting risk
	•	Promote a culture to facilitate smooth risk management process for compliance and financial reporting risk
	•	Review and approve the Converged Risk Assessment Framework
	•	Ensure seamless implementation of the framework across the Bank 
	•	Interact with regulators to discuss matters related to regulatory compliance which may pose a significant compliance risk for the Bank
	•	Promote a compliance culture within the Bank 
	•	Coordination for identification and management of the Bank’s compliance risk
	•	Act as a nodal point of contact between the Bank and the regulators
	•	Design, implement and maintain the Converged Risk Assessment framework
	•	Facilitate trainings on compliance risks and its importance
	•	Approve and implement the procedures for effective communication of compliance expectations within the Bank
	•	Head of Compliance and ICOFR
	•	Develop the Converged Risk Assessment framework 
	•	Define the roles and responsibilities of the Compliance Monitoring team and ICOFR Compliance team
	•	Head of Compliance Process Re-Engineering
	•	Facilitate technology enablement for implementation of the framework

	•	Components of Converged Risk Assessment framework 

4.1 Assessment Units (AU) Identification and mapping

4.1.1 Defining AUs 
For performing convergent risk assessment, the user groups within the Bank will be segregated into various units called ‘Assessment Units’ based on key business groups and support functions. Each assessment unit will be determined on the basis of:
	•	Current business alignment
	•	Alignment and specialization of Compliance Group 
	•	Current business performance and monitoring 
	•	Management oversight and control
The AUs will be aligned to the prevailing Management Organization (MO) structure. 
The Head - ICOFR and Compliance Monitoring team will be responsible for determining any changes in the list of AUs as and when required. The list of AUs will be reviewed on an Annual basis. An earlier evaluation of assessment units may be done under exceptional circumstances basis mid-year changes in organization and reporting structures.
Any modification to the list of AUs will be reviewed by the Head of Compliance and ICOFR monitoring.
The Compliance Technology team coordinator will be responsible for enabling the corresponding updates in the SAS GCM system.
For a detailed listing of Assessment Units (AUs) as per the current Management Organization (MO) structure, refer Annexure I
4.1.2 Cluster Identification
The compliance universe consisting of various internal policies or external regulations, acts, statutes, etc. will be different for each Assessment Unit. Hence, it will be appropriate to assess the Compliance risk profile of each Assessment Unit based on the regulations with which it needs to comply.  
In order to complete the risk assessment within a reasonable time and to arrive at a meaningful output, it is necessary to have a repository of applicable regulatory circulars and corresponding compliance obligations for each AU. 
The regulatory circulars will be group into clusters based on the following:
	•	Master directions and their applicability to products/ processes
	•	New regulations 
	•	Common compliance/ risk themes
Considering the Management Organization (MO) structure of the Bank, one cluster of regulations may be applicable to one or more Assessment Units (AUs). 
Thus, it will be imperative to define the list of regulatory circulars applicable to an AU.

4.1.3 Circular to Compliance theme mapping

The purpose of theme-based reviews is to gather sufficient documented evidence to enable a conclusion and testimony as to whether or not the controls as documented are operating in practice. Thus, theme-based risk review involves on-site evaluation of actual transactions and controls by the Compliance testing unit, to gain sufficient assurance that the prescribed internal controls are operating effectively. 
Theme based risk review is important for the following reasons:
	•	Identification of potential weaknesses in terms of risk of non-compliance and facilitation of further improvements of the compliance risk controls.
	•	Mitigation of weaknesses identified
	•	As a key input for Compliance Risk Assessment
The theme-based review plan enables the Compliance Group to understand and articulate the themes / AUs/ regulatory clusters / controls to be reviewed along with the frequency of review. The size and scale of the bank’s operations, complexity of products involved, jurisdictions in which the Bank is operating etc. necessitate the review plan to be developed in a risk sensitive manner. 
A risk-based approach provides flexibility in use of compliance resources whilst also being able to add value by ensuring leading practices are being implemented across the businesses. Thus, the aim of this approach is to:
	•	Ensure that the overall intensity of the theme-based risk review plan is proportionate to the compliance risk that each AU poses for the bank
	•	Provide reasonable assurance to senior management and regulators that key potential regulatory risks are being assessed and appropriately actioned. 
The preparation of thematic review plan and the timelines for the same will be decided by the compliance and ICOFR team which will be subjected to review by the CG Advisory Group Head to identify any modifications required to bring the same in line with changes, if any, in the overall business / regulatory environment. Major changes to the plan should be carried out in consultation with the Group Chief Compliance Officer (GCCO) of the Bank. Further, the ICOFR and Compliance monitoring team should also document changes to the plan along with underlying rationale for the same.
	•	Each of the regulatory circular will be mapped to one of the following regulatory themes based on the underlying nature of the compliance: Complexity of product/ process to which regulation pertains
	•	Emerging regulatory focus areas identified through
	•	Risk based supervision by the RBI
	•	New regulatory themes
	•	Repeat internal/ external audit findings
	•	External fraud events
	•	Compliance breach reports
Each of the regulatory circular will be mapped to one of the regulatory themes present in the annexure based on the underlying nature of the compliance requirements.

The CG Advisory Group Heads in conjunction with Business Heads will be responsible for determining the Compliance themes. and will present to the Group Compliance Officer for his approval. Post approval from the GCCO, the same will be presented to the ACM. The list of themes will be reviewed on Annually. However, in case of any external events, the themes can be reviewed on a need basis. 
Any modifications to the list of themes will be reviewed by the Head of Compliance and ICOFR monitoring. and approved by the Group Compliance Officer. Post approval from the GCCO, the same will be presented to the ACM.
Post approval from the Group Compliance Officer, the Compliance Technology team coordinator will be responsible for enabling the corresponding updates in the SAS GCM system.

4.1.5 Rationalization of Risks and Controls (RCMs) at AU level

For the purpose of converged risk assessment, it will be imperative to identify the compliance and financial reporting risk at an Assessment Unit (AU) level. To achieve this objective, the existing repository of ICOFR RCMs will be leveraged and rationalized to arrive at a comprehensive listing of applicable Financial risks and controls. The Compliance RCM is built as part of the converged and rationalized RCMs. The rationalization will take into consideration the following:
	•	Using the compliance obligations inventory applicable to the AU, compliance risks will be identified
	•	Existing RCMs applicable to the AU will be enhanced to incorporate missing compliance risks, if any
	•	Operational risks not having any impact from financial reporting (ICOFR) perspective and any other redundant risks and controls will be eliminated
Converged RCMs will be documented such that compliance and financial reporting risk having common controls are clubbed and risk assessment can be carried out at a common ground.
4.1.6 Change Management and Periodic reviews of RCM

	•	The converged RCM shall be updated by the respective assessment units on the basis of triggers such as regulatory notifications, changes in internal policies and processes (processes are reviewed once in 1,2- or 3-years basis importance). 
	•	The changes in compliance obligations and attributes of risk and control will be reviewed and approved by the Head - ICOFR and Compliance Monitoring team.
	•	The periodic reviews of RCMs shall be conducted as per Bank’s policy.

	•	Risk Assessment 

 The risk assessment will be system generated basis auto-trigger during the quarter January to March every year. The results of this assessment will be used for planning the Compliance testing and ICOFR monitoring activities.
In case of disagreements on the Risk Assessment with the AU heads, the ICOFR and Compliance Monitoring team member shall document the rationale of Risk Assessment, including points of difference, and discuss the same with the Chief Compliance Officer. Based on the feedback from the Chief Compliance Officer, the Inherent Risk Assessment would be finalized.
	•	Inherent risk assessment
Inherent risk is defined as ‘the status of risk (measured through impact and/or likelihood) without considering any direct controls that the Bank may already have in place’.  In other words, it means the risk embedded in the business/ operations undertaken by the Bank without considering the control environment.
The inherent risk assessment is done using a Top-Down approach, whereby the assessment is done at an AU level for various compliance/ risk themes. 

The rating score of likelihood and impact is multiplied to arrive at the inherent risk score. This assessment helps determine which AUs are riskier than others on account of the inherent nature/ complexity of their products or processes and within an individual AU, which clusters of regulations are riskier. 
	•	Likelihood assessment
Likelihood is the probability of occurrence of risk. Following parameters are evaluated for arriving at the likelihood score of an Assessment Unit (AU) for a definite period, 
	•	Business/ transaction volumes 
	•	Complexity or changes in products and systems (new product/ system roll-out)
	•	No. of regulatory returns (Critical/ non-critical returns/ feeder units to regulatory returns)
	•	Number of compliance breaches observed
	•	Number of ICOFR control failures 
	•	No. of Customer complaints
Since the above parameters are applicable for an assessment unit as a whole, the assessment of likelihood of occurrence would be assessed for at assessment unit level as whole. The above-mentioned parameters need to be rated for each AU based on the following pattern:

4.2.3 Materiality Assessment for ICOFR

As per International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS),” Information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence the decisions that the primary users of general purpose financial statements make on the basis of those financial statements, which provide financial information about a specific reporting entity.”. As per the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB) Auditing Standard, “The risk of material misstatement refers to the risk that the financial statements are materially misstated. Auditing Standard No. 12, Identifying and Assessing Risks of Material Misstatement, indicates that the auditor should assess the risks of material misstatement at two levels: (1) at the financial statement level and (2) at the assertion level.”. Materiality analysis refers to the identification and prioritization of issues which will have a significant impact on the business of the Bank and its stakeholders. It provides for an opportunity to review the existing products, processes and business groups, along with corresponding risks and improve the underlying business strategy. 

Below will be the key steps to perform materiality assessment:

	•	Determining the materiality level and tolerable error
	•	Identifying of significant accounts based on quantitative and qualitative factors
	•	Mapping significant accounts to products, business groups, processes

In order to determine significant accounts, materiality should be defined at two levels:
	•	At the overall financial statements level (as it relates to the consolidated financial statements taken as a whole)
	•	The individual account level (pertaining to a group of accounts or segments of business or captions for subsidiaries/ groups/ entities of the Bank)
Overall materiality involves the risk of material misstatement of the consolidated financial statements. Overall materiality is used to assess the aggregated misstatements. The parameters which will be used to calculate the overall materiality is mentioned in ““Determination of materiality level and tolerable error” section.
Determination of materiality level and tolerable error
For the purpose of review of internal control over financial reporting (ICOFR) and compliance, the materiality level will be defined as 5% of profit before tax (PBT) of the “standalone” Bank for revenue items and 0.5% of total assets of the standalone bank. A further haircut of 25% is to be considered thereon for arriving at the overall materiality limit.
Tolerable error will be defined as per bank’s internal tolerable threshold.
Identification of significant accounts
The key inputs to identification of significant accounts will be as under:
	•	Chart of accounts
	•	Trial balance
	•	Zero balance ledger accounts 
	•	List of significant locations/ branches
	•	Adjustment entries
	•	Notes to accounts
For identifying significant accounts, the following quantitative analysis will be performed:
	•	Review trial balance covering:
	•	G/L (Level 1)
	•	Head (Level 2)
	•	Account (Level 3)
	•	Based on materiality threshold and tolerable error level, significant accounts will be identified at “Account” level
	•	Further, for accounts not identified as significant, aggregation will be done at “Head” level and thresholds will be applied to identify additional significant accounts, if any.
Further, non-significant accounts based on quantitative analysis and zero balance accounts will further be reviewed to for qualitative factors such as:
	•	Purpose of the account
	•	Nature of entries
	•	Volume and complexity of transactions
	•	Susceptibility to fraud
Mapping of significant accounts 
The significant accounts will be mapped to products, business groups and processes.

This assessment will involve:

	•	Control performance assessment, wherein an assessment of the operating effectiveness of processes is performed.

	•	Residual risk at AU level
Residual risk is defined as ‘risk remaining after considering the controls put in place to mitigate the inherent risk’. It indicates the effectiveness of controls in mitigating the inherent risk i.e. for an assessment unit, if the controls put in place to mitigate the inherent risk are designed and operating effectively, its residual risk would be lower. Further, data for each of the parameter will be taken with one quarter lag i.e., for March’22 Residual Risk assessment all the data will be considered from Apr’21 to Dec’21.

Rating override

The ratings described till now are arrived on the basis of a model, however, there may be some factors based on which the Compliance Officer/ ICOFR Officer may want to increase or decrease on account of their expert judgement, ground realities or recent regulatory activity on a particular topic. 
The rating may be modified based on certain ‘overriding factors’ described below. However, the reason for the override shall be recorded by GCCO/ICOFR officer and approved by the Head of Compliance and ICOFR Monitoring. 
Causes that can initiate the rating override:
Warning to other players in the industry/competitors: Regulators may warn the industry or the competitors in the industry, on the practices followed by the banks. This could raise the risk level for the Bank and hence, reconsideration of the residual risk rating may be required
Litigation: Significant lawsuit against a Business Group (or similar business at another bank in the same market) during the year may raise a concern and hence one may want to override the risk rating and alter the monitoring plan.
Any other factors: The above-mentioned factors are indicative in nature. There could be several other factors that an experienced assessor may consider overriding the residual risk rating.
